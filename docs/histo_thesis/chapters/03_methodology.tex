This chapter describes how the system processes a corpus of histopathology papers, and converts them into a set of structured biomedical claims, each of which can be traced back to which paper and paragraph it was taken from. The system consists of two pipelines, connected by a relational database: a document-extraction pipeline, which parses each PDF into text elements, figures, tables, the cross-references between them, while preserving provenance; and a knowledge-extraction pipeline, which decomposes the extracted text into atomic findings, and refines them through grounding, normalization, grouping, canonicalization, relation classification and scoring stages. 

At the core of the knowledge-extraction pipeline is the MAP stage with agreement-based cascading: a cascade that routes text chunks between voter tiers of different quality and cost, according to how strongly independent voters at a certain tier agree; and it decides whether to accept a cheap-tier output, to reject the voter outputs, or to escalate the text chunk to a more capable (but expensive) tier. The system is designed to surface traceable rule candidates for expert review; it does not aim to produce clinical rules autonomously.

This chapter follows the flow of data throughout the system. Section~\ref{section:system_overview} presents the end-to-end architecture, and the principles that were followed in the design and the implementation: reproducibility, provenance, and modularity. Section~\ref{section:Corpus} describes the corpus drawn from PubMed Central Open Access Subset, and the datasets used for calibration and evaluation of the pipeline. Then, Sections~\ref{section:Document_Extraction} and~\ref{section:Knowledge_Extraction} explain the document- and knowledge-extraction pipelines. Later, in Section~\ref{section:Agreement_Based_Cascading}, the agreement-based cascading mechanism that is used at the first stage of the knowledge-extraction pipeline is described in detail. Finally, Section~\ref{section:Evaluation_Methodology} defines how the four research questions of Table~\ref{tab:research-questions} were measured: document-extraction faithfulness (RQ1), knowledge-extraction accuracy and provenance carry-rate (RQ2), cascade cost-quality trade-off (RQ3), and the behaviour of the grounding and relation classification gates (RQ4).
