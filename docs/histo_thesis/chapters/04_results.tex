\chapter{Results}\label{chapter:Results}

This chapter reports the empirical findings of the thesis, in the order of the system architecture. Section~\ref{section:results-doc-extraction-performance} analyses the document extraction quality (RQ1); Section~\ref{section:results-ke-pipeline-output-and-provenance} analyses the output of each stage in the knowledge extraction pipeline and how much provenance has been preserved (RQ2); Section~\ref{section:results-abc-calibration} explains the calibration results of the agreement-based cascading system; Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison} explains the cost-quality comparison, which is the central result for RQ3. Section~\ref{section:results-nli-in-grounding-and-relation-classification} investigates the two NLI components -- the grounding filter and the relation classification -- and answers RQ4. Finally, Section~\ref{section:results-generalization-to-heldout-papers} reports the findings of the generalization test of RQ5, run on a held-out evaluation set.

Each reported number is tied to its corresponding metric definition in Section~\ref{section:Evaluation_Methodology}. Each comparison is reported either with confidence intervals, or explicitly marked as descriptive. All knowledge-extraction and cascade results are produced by replaying cached voter artifacts as described in Subsection~\ref{subsec:abc-calibration-and-replay}, so that no API calls are made to models during the experimentation process. 

The overall result is mixed. Document-extraction (Section~\ref{section:results-doc-extraction-performance}) and provenance preservation (Section~\ref{section:results-ke-pipeline-output-and-provenance}) meet their intended targets. The agreement cascade reaches the quality of the strong single-model baseline (Sonnet 4.6) and significantly outperforms each cheaper model, but shows no statistically significant strict-F1 improvement over the strongest single model.

Since the cascade is more expensive to run, the usage of the cascade over a single strong model is not cost-justified; Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison} reports the RQ3 results directly, while Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion} argues that the cascade's value lies in the reusable framework it provides. The NLI components of Section~\ref{section:results-nli-in-grounding-and-relation-classification} behave as designed, and the calibrated configuration of the system generalizes cleanly to the held-out evaluation set, as reported in Section~\ref{section:results-generalization-to-heldout-papers}.
