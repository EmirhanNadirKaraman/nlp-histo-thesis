\section{Research questions}\label{section:intro-research-questions}

This thesis is structured around five research questions, listed in Table~\ref{tab:research-questions} and evaluated in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Results}. Each of the questions are quantitatively evaluated with concrete measurements. 

\medskip{}

\textbf{RQ1 -- Document extraction.} How faithfully does the document-extraction pipeline recover the text, tables, figures, captions, and related layout information from the papers? This question also asks how much improvement the proposed pipeline provides, compared to the off-the-shelf document-extractor baseline; the measurements are made with a hand-annotated rubric over a set of papers.

\medskip{}

\textbf{RQ2 -- Knowledge extraction and provenance.} What is the quality of the extracted findings and rules against strong-model generated silver labels, and is provenance preserved end-to-end for every extracted figure, table, and rule?

\medskip{}

\textbf{RQ3 -- Cost-quality trade-off.} Is the agreement-based cascade worth its cost, relative to sending each text chunk to a single strong model?

\medskip{}

\textbf{RQ4 -- Grounding and relation classification.} How does the shared biomedical NLI model~\parencite{gu2021biomedbert} perform in its grounding and relation classification roles?

\medskip{}

\textbf{RQ5 -- Held-out generalization.} Does the performance of the configuration calibrated on one set of papers generalize to a held-out set, or does it overfit to the calibration cluster?

\medskip{}

There is a common methodological caveat, threatening the validity of the results for RQ2-RQ5. The silver metric used to answer these questions depend on the silver labels generated by a strong LLM, meaning that the resulting scores measure agreement with the labeler, not ground truth. This follows the recent work that assesses systems based on silver labels produced by another model~\parencite{}. The explained caveat is stated as the main validity threat in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion}.
