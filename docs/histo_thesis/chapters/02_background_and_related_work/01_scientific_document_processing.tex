\section{Scientific document processing}\label{section:background-scientific-doc-processing}

The thesis operates on histopathology papers in English, downloaded from the PubMed Central Open Access Subset~\parencite{nlm_pmc_open_access}. PDF, the common distribution format used in scientific literature, is designed for visual representation rather than for conveying logical structure; therefore, the section hierarchy, reading order, tables, figures, captions, and their relationships should be reconstructed~\parencite{wang2024detectorderconstructtreeconstruction, meunier2005optimizedxycutdetermining, chen2025graphbaseddocumentstructure}. The recovery of these elements is the task of document layout analysis. Large datasets drawn from scientific literature have been developed to support research in this area. For example, PubLayNet aligns XML annotations with more than one million PubMed Central PDFs, while DocBank provides token-level annotations for document-layout analysis~\parencite{zhong2019publaynetlargestdataset, li2020docbankbenchmarkdataset}.

This reconstruction is especially important in a biomedical setting: clinically relevant claims are not confined to running text; they may appear in tables, figures, captions, or footnotes. A pipeline that uses only body text therefore risks discarding part of the evidence it is later asked to cite and ground~\parencite{subramanian2020medicatdatasetmedical, oshima2024syntheticcontextllm}. The pipeline treats the recovery of text, sections, figures, tables, captions, footnotes, and the bounding box coordinates for tables and figures as a first-class stage (§\ref{section:Document_Extraction}) rather than an afterthought. It then carries this source information forward, so that every downstream claim can be traced back to its source, and every figure or table to its page and coordinates.
