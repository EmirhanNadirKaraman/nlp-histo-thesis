\section{Evaluation of LLM extraction systems}\label{section:background-evaluation-of-llm-extraction-systems}

The reference labels are not human-annotated, but are also generated by an LLM. When human gold annotations are not available, the recent work uses silver labels generated by strong models as a scalable alternative, but this introduces a clear risk: an evaluation process based on silver labels can inherit the biases, preferences, or the failure modes of the model that generates the labels~\parencite{}.

This thesis uses a human-labeled rubric for document extraction, and strong-model silver labels generated by Opus 4.7 for the scoring and evaluation of knowledge extraction. In particular, strict-F1 used for evaluation measures agreement with the silver labeler, not clinical truth, and the shared model family between models in the cascade and the silver labeler might inflate the measured agreement scores. The limitations this situation causes are explicitly noted in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion}. 

This thesis makes two methodological contributions. First, an offline-replay protocol is used for evaluation, making it possible to reproduce every reported result from cached artifacts without additional model calls. Second, a cost-quality frontier reports the strict-F1 scores for multiple operating points (economy/knee/quality), rather than a single score, making the trade-off between cost, quality, and model-routing decisions visible.
