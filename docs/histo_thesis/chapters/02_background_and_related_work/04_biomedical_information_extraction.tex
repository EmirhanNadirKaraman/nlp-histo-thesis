\section{Biomedical information extraction}\label{section:background-biomedical-info-extraction}

The goal of biomedical information extraction is to turn biomedical text in the papers into a structured representation of entities, relations, and claims. This is a long-standing task, and approaches to solve this problem range from rule-based systems and supervised relation extraction to more recent LLM-based extraction methods~\parencite{agrawal2022largelanguagemodels, gu2021biomedbert}. The thesis follows a similar path, but differs from such classical information extraction approaches in two important ways. 

First, the unit of output in this thesis is an atomic, typed finding: a subject-outcome assertion with an explicit relation type, direction, and scope. This representation allows findings to be normalized, grouped, and compared (§\ref{section:Knowledge_Extraction}). Second, every finding is linked to the exact source paragraph it was extracted from, in order to preserve provenance during the knowledge extraction process (§\ref{section:background-provenance-traceability-and-auditability}). Histopathology suits this design: its claims are dense and entity-rich, and they often compare diseases, marker patterns, or morphological features, making the domain a good test bed for structured, auditable claim extraction.
