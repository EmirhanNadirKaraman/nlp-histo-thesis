\section{Biomedical information extraction}\label{section:background-biomedical-info-extraction}

The goal of biomedical information extraction is to turn biomedical text in the papers to a structured representation of entities, relations, and claims. This is a long-standing task, and approaches to solve this problem range from rule-based systems and supervised relation extraction to more recent LLM-based extraction methods~\parencite{}. The thesis follows a similar path, but differs from such classical information extraction approaches in two important ways. 

First, the unit of output in this thesis is an atomic, typed finding, rather than a free-text summary: a subject-outcome assertion with an explicit relation type, direction, and scope. This representation allows for findings to be normalized, grouped, and compared to each other (Section~\ref{section:Knowledge_Extraction}). Second, every finding is linked to the exact source paragraph it was extracted from, in order to preserve provenance during the knowledge extraction process. Histopathology is a suitable domain for this design, as the claims extracted from its papers are dense, entity-rich, and often compare the disease, marker pattern, or morphological features they incorporate with each other, making them suitable for testing structured and auditable claim extraction.
