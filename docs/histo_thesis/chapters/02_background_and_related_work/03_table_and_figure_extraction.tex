\section{Table and figure extraction}\label{section:background-table-and-figure-extraction}

Figures, and especially tables, are the parts of a paper that are the most difficult for a layout parser to recover. However, the central quantitative evidence is presented in such formats in histopathology papers. Table extraction is commonly divided into two separate tasks: table detection and table structure recognition. Table detection involves locating tables and their boundaries, whereas table structure recognition involves reconstructing rows, columns, and cells. Earlier systems often relied on layout heuristics, statistical methods, or symbolic reasoning rather than end-to-end learning. This includes work that reconstructed the functions and relationships of cells in clinical-trial tables, or combined deep learning with symbolic analysis to determine exact table boundaries~\parencite{milosevic2016disentanglingstructuretables, zhang2021integratedapproachdeep}. Recent work on these tasks increasingly uses image-based transformer detectors~\parencite{smock2022pubtables1mcomprehensivetable, smock2025pubtablesv2newlargescale}. Figure extraction and the association of captions to figures follow a similar line, with systems designed to detect figures, extract their captions, and associate them correctly across domains ranging from biomedical to physics literature~\parencite{clark2015extractingfigurestables, lopez2011automaticsystemextracting, praczyk2013automaticextractionfigures, raychoudhury2015automaticextractionfigures, siegel2018extractingscientificfigures, li2019figurecaptionextraction, chaki2023automaticsystemextracting}. Dedicated benchmarks are used to evaluate progress in figure, table, and caption extraction. SCI-3000, for example, contains annotations for figures, tables, and captions across 3\,000 scientific PDFs from five disciplines. Its evaluation procedure matches predicted regions to ground-truth, and then evaluates the correctness of caption associations~\parencite{darmanovic2023sci3000datasetfigure}.

More recent work moves beyond geometric figure--caption pairing, and studies how figures relate to the surrounding body text that discusses them. Such methods learn document-level cross-modal representations to identify which passages are most relevant to a figure, rather than treating each image-caption pair in isolation~\parencite{liu2024documentcliplinkingfigures}. The thesis follows the same decomposition. The document-extraction stage combines a layout-based detector (Docling) with an image-based table detector (Microsoft's Table Transformer) into a hybrid detector and adds footnote expansion, geometric caption matching, size-based icon filtering, and sub-figure merging, as described in Section~\ref{section:Document_Extraction}. Section~\ref{section:results-doc-extraction-performance} shows that off-the-shelf Docling already localizes tables well and finds every figure but over-emits decorative icons; the improvements over that baseline come from these post-processing steps.
