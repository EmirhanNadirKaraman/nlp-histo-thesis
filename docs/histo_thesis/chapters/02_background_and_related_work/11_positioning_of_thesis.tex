\section{Positioning of this thesis}\label{section:background-positioning-of-thesis}

This thesis builds on the mature components that are surveyed in the preceding sections, but differs in how they are combined: its position is that an auditable clinical knowledge base requires an integrated pipeline that preserves end-to-end provenance, and evaluates quality together with cost, addressing the gap identified at the beginning of this chapter.

The system therefore consists of two pipelines: first, a provenance-preserving document-extraction stage built on Docling, with tables detected by merging its table regions with a table transformer (TATR)~\parencite{}, and post-processing; and second, a grounded, atomic, and normalized rule-extraction pipeline using scispaCy and UMLS with a frozen biomedical NLI backbone~\parencite{neumann2019scispacy, bodenreider2004umls, gu2021biomedbert, romanov2018mednli}, whose MAP stage uses an agreement-based cascade~\parencite{kolawole2025abc} that is calibrated on a histopathology corpus.

These components are tied together by the evaluation: the thesis measures what it costs to keep every extracted rule traceable to its evidence, where existing components are already sufficient off-the-shelf, and where additional stages in the pipeline, such as grounding, agreement, and escalation, are worth their cost. The results are reported and discussed in Chapters~\ref{chapter:Results} and~\ref{chapter:Discussion}.
