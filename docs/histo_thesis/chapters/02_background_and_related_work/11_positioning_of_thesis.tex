\section{Positioning of this thesis}\label{section:background-positioning-of-thesis}

The preceding sections describe components that each have their own literature, but are usually treated separately: table, figure, and text extraction from PDFs, biomedical entity normalization, LLM-based extraction, natural-language inference, and cost-aware model routing. The position of this thesis is that an auditable clinical knowledge base cannot be built from any of these components alone; they must be integrated into a single pipeline that preserves end-to-end provenance, in which quality is evaluated together with cost. 

The system therefore consists of three parts. First, it uses a provenance-preserving document extraction stage built on Docling, with tables detected by merging its table regions with a table transformer (TATR)~\parencite{}, and post-processing. Second, it implements a grounded, atomic, and normalized rule-extraction pipeline using scispaCy and UMLS with a frozen biomedical NLI backbone~\parencite{neumann2019scispacy, bodenreider2004umls, gu2021biomedbert, romanov2018mednli}. Third, it adds an agreement-based cascade~\parencite{kolawole2025abc} that is calibrated on a histopathology corpus. 

These components are tied together by the evaluation: the thesis measures what it costs to keep every extracted rule traceable to its evidence, where existing components are already sufficient off-the-shelf, and where additional stages in the pipeline, such as grounding, agreement, and escalation, are worth their cost. The results are reported and discussed in Chapters~\ref{chapter:Results} and~\ref{chapter:Discussion}.
