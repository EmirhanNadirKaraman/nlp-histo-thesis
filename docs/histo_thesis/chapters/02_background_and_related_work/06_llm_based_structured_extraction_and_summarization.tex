\section{LLM-based structured extraction and summarization}\label{section:background-llm-based-structured-extraction-and-summarization}

Large language models are increasingly used to read text, and emit structured outputs under a predefined schema, which is also the mechanism that is used in the MAP stage of this thesis. Two properties of LLM-based extraction are important for the pipeline design of the thesis. 

First, a single LLM call is not reliable enough by itself and its output should be checked, as it may hallucinate, paraphrase evidence too freely, or produce different outputs across different runs. For these reasons, agreement-based strategies, such as self-consistency are used in the literature, where multiple output generations are collected, and compared or reconciled, to get a more reliable version of the output~\parencite{}. This thesis generalizes this idea by extending repeated output generation from the same model to cross-provider voting, with the agreement-based cascading mechanism of Section~\ref{section:Knowledge_Extraction}. 

Second, LLM outputs are easier to evaluate and trust, when they are typed and linked to an evidence, as opposed to free-form text~\parencite{}. For this reason, the thesis favors typed, atomic findings over summarization-style consolidation of free-text findings~\parencite{}: atomic claims are more easily grounded against their evidence, normalized against biomedical entities, and audited.
