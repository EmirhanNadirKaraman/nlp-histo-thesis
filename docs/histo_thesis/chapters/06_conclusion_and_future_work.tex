\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{chapter:Conclusion_and_Future_Work}

The goal of the thesis was to turn unstructured histopathology papers in PDF format into a structured, source-faithful, and auditable clinical knowledge base, where each output can be traced back to the evidence that produced it. To do this, the thesis built two pipelines. The first is a provenance-preserving document-extraction pipeline that converts PMC Open Access PDFs into structured text, figures, and tables; the second is a knowledge-extraction pipeline that turns the structured text output of document-extraction into atomic, grounded, and canonical rules. The system is evaluated against five research questions; the results are reported and discussed in Chapters~\ref{chapter:Results} and~\ref{chapter:Discussion}.

The pipeline reliably extracts figures and tables, preserves the provenance chain for every artifact at each stage, and produces a large rule base, with each rule backed by its source, that generalizes to held-out papers without clear signs of overfitting. There is one negative result in the thesis, stated directly: the agreement cascade reaches the same quality as the best single model but is not cost-justified relative to it; however, the cascade still outperforms the weaker single models. The overall design of the system favors source-faithfulness and traceability over raw silver-agreement: exactly the right priorities for a clinical knowledge base.

The lasting contribution is therefore not the exact calibration configuration or the optimal operating point in the cascade; it is the framework itself, which can be adapted to different contexts: with a different corpus or voter pool, and different budget constraints. The remainder of this chapter summarizes the contributions of the thesis (Section~\ref{section:contributions}), and the open problems left for future work, especially why an evaluation process against human labels is necessary, rather than silver labels (Section~\ref{section:future-work}).
