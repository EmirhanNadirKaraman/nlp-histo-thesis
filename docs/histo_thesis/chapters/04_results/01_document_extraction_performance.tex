\section{Document extraction performance}\label{section:results-doc-extraction-performance}

The document-extraction pipeline is evaluated on the 27-PDF rubric set, described in Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-datasets}. The evaluation uses the four rubric dimensions, and reports the strict-F1 performance, where an extracted element is counted as a true positive only if it scores the top label for each rubric dimension. Both the rubric dimensions and the strict-F1 definitions are explained in Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-doc-extraction}.

The main improvement is observed for table extraction. Compared with the Docling baseline, strict-F1 rises from 40\% to 83.8\% for tables in the hybrid variant with footnote expand multiplier set to $\times1.2$. Docling does not detect table footnotes at all: Docling's footnote precision result of 43.8\% comes from tables that do not have footnotes, and are labelled as correct by the rubric. The strict-F1 improvement was driven mainly by footnote expansion, with an increase in footnote precision accuracy from 43.8\% to 91.4\%.

Crop and bounding-box detections were strong in the baseline, with a 91.4\% crop F1, and a 94.3\% mask F1; the bottleneck was in including the footnotes in the table crop, and extending the detected table crop downward (footnote-expand $\times1.2$) over the hybrid detector more than doubled the strict-F1 score of the baseline. 

Since figures do not have footnotes, the footnote dimension of the rubric was disabled for figure strict-F1 calculations. Docling's performance was good enough for the goals of this thesis, so no changes were made to Docling's default figure detection behavior. Therefore the reported results do not change across variants. The production configuration has an 89.9\% crop F1, and a 84.0\% strict-F1, as reported in Table~\ref{tab:figure-emission-metrics}.

The remaining table-failure modes are caption-attachment errors and cropping errors. Caption-attachment errors are caused by continuation markers parsed as separate tables, tables spanning multiple pages, rotated tables, or captions attached to the side or corner of the tables. Cropping errors are caused by multiple tables on the same page, side-by-side, causing failures due to not being able to detect the boundaries of the tables, erroneously detecting a single table. 

The observed figure-failure modes are mostly caused by icons and in-text decorative images. Although these artifacts are falsely detected as figures, they are still masked, and the text in them does not bleed into the main content. Therefore, they do not cause an issue for the remainder of the pipeline, and solutions for this problem were not investigated further. Nevertheless, removing these artifacts is the route to obtaining higher F1 scores, and is noted as a possible future improvement. These results address \textbf{RQ1}.
