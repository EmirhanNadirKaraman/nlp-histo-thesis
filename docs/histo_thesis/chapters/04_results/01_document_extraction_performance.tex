\section{Document extraction performance}\label{section:results-doc-extraction-performance}

The document-extraction pipeline is evaluated on the 27-PDF rubric set, described in Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-datasets}. The evaluation uses the four rubric dimensions, and reports the strict-F1 performance, where an extracted element is counted as a true positive only if it scores the top label for each rubric dimension. Then, the production configuration for document-extraction was selected by the staged configuration sweep. Rubric dimensions, strict-F1 definitions, and the staged sweep are explained in detail in Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-doc-extraction}. 

The best configuration for table extraction was found to be a hybrid configuration, from which artifacts were extracted by unioning Docling and Microsoft TATR table detector. The achieved table-extraction metrics for the most important variants, as well as the figure-extraction metrics across all variants, are shown in Tables~\ref{tab:table-emission-metrics} and~\ref{tab:figure-emission-metrics}.

The v01 baseline in Table~\ref{tab:table-emission-metrics} is not off-the-shelf Docling: it is the full document-extraction pipeline with its masking, caption matching, icon and size filtering, and cropping stages; this variant runs the whole document-extraction pipeline, where Docling-only table detection and the table-handling options are disabled. Docling's figure and table detection is used as is, but the surrounding structure supporting the Docling output is applied for all variants other than the off-the-shelf Docling. The off-the-shelf Docling variant strips the scaffolding away, so that its results can be compared to the other variants, and the effect of the scaffolding around it can be shown.

The main improvement is observed for table extraction. Compared with off-the-shelf Docling, strict table-F1 rises from $36.6\%$ to $83.8\%$ in the hybrid variant with footnote expand multiplier set to $\times1.2$ (most of it over the v01 pipeline baseline, which already reaches $40.0\%$. Docling does not detect table footnotes at all: Docling's footnote precision result of 43.8\% comes from tables that do not have footnotes, and are labeled as correct by the rubric. The strict-F1 improvement was driven mainly by footnote expansion, with an increase in footnote precision from 43.8\% to 91.4\%.

% Compared with the Docling-only pipeline baseline, strict-F1 rises from 40\% to 83.8\% for tables in the hybrid variant with footnote expand multiplier set to $\times1.2$. Docling does not detect table footnotes at all: Docling's footnote precision result of 43.8\% comes from tables that do not have footnotes, and are labelled as correct by the rubric. The strict-F1 improvement was driven mainly by footnote expansion, with an increase in footnote precision from 43.8\% to 91.4\%.

Crop and bounding-box detections were strong in the baseline, with a 91.4\% crop F1, and a 94.3\% mask F1; the bottleneck was in including the footnotes in the table crop, and extending the detected table crop downward (footnote-expand $\times1.2$) over the hybrid detector more than doubled the strict-F1 score of the baseline. This came at a small cost in mask-F1 ($94.3\% \rightarrow 91.9\%$), due to the downward crop expansion for footnote detection occasionally over-masking the region just below the table crop. 

The off-the-shelf Docling rows separate the effect of the pipeline scaffold from the table-handling sweep. Off-the-shelf Docling achieves $36.6\%$ strict-F1, which is slightly below the v01 pipeline baseline, at $40.0\%$ strict-F1 -- a 3-point gain from the pipeline's caption matching. Both configurations nevertheless fail the strict table rubric for the same reason: neither captures the table footnotes. This also explains the mask-F1 pattern: configurations without footnote expansion score highest on mask-F1 because crops are never extended downward to detect footnotes, eliminating the errors caused by over-masking. Mask-F1 rewards doing less, while strict-F1 exposes the footnote detection failure that decides the evaluation.

Figures are handled differently. Docling's figure detection was sufficient for the goals of this thesis, and was therefore not changed. For this reason, the figure metrics do not change across the table-handling sweep variants. The footnote dimension is also disabled, as figures do not carry footnotes. After figure detection, the pipeline post-processes every figure: icon and size filtering and sub-figure merging cut emitted crops from 139 to 76, decorative icon false positives decrease from 73 to 14, and caption matching increases the share of figures with correct captions from $60.6\%$ to $90.3\%$. All of these changes together raise the figure strict-F1 from off-the-shelf Docling's $44.7\%$ to $84.0\%$ for the production configuration, as reported in Table~\ref{tab:figure-emission-metrics}.

The remaining table-failure modes are caption-attachment errors and cropping errors. Caption-attachment errors are caused by continuation markers parsed as separate tables, tables spanning multiple pages, rotated tables, or captions attached to the side or corner of the tables. Cropping errors are caused by multiple tables on the same page, side-by-side, causing failures due to not being able to detect the boundaries of the tables, erroneously detecting a single table. 

The observed figure-failure modes are mostly caused by icons and in-text decorative images. Although these artifacts are falsely detected as figures, they are still masked, and the text in them does not bleed into the main content. Therefore, they do not cause an issue for the remainder of the pipeline, and solutions for this problem were not investigated further. Nevertheless, removing these artifacts is the route to obtaining higher F1 scores, and is noted as a possible future improvement. These results address RQ1.
