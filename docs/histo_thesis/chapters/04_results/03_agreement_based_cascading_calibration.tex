\section{Agreement-based cascading calibration}\label{section:results-abc-calibration}

This section describes the configuration selection process for RQ3 for the agreement-based cascading, used in the MAP-stage. The calibration is done on the 15-paper related subset, and it fixes the configuration used in the downstream pipeline. The cost-quality implications of the configuration, including the results of cascade structures with different voter model subsets, are evaluated separately in Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison}.

All calibration results reported in this section are produced by offline replay. The voter outputs are generated once, stored in a primer cache, and different cascade configurations are evaluated by replaying the cached outputs; during the calibration sweeps, no additional API calls are made, so the process is completely offline. The \texttt{related15} calibration set contains 15 papers; 454 cases are generated from these papers, and the cascade produces 2\,294 findings from these cases. Then, the cascade outputs are scored against Opus silver labels and a strict-F1 value is calculated. An optimal (Hungarian) one-to-one matcher is used for the scoring; the scores calculated using a greedy one-to-one matcher are also reported, but only as a sensitivity diagnostic; the greedy matcher never drives selection. The results in this section are in-sample; an unbiased out-of-sample generalization test on held-out papers is deferred to Section~\ref{section:results-generalization-to-heldout-papers}.

\medskip{}

\textbf{Calibration design.} The cascade has many knobs: embedder, similarity scorer, alignment strategy, continuous weights of the scorers, the voter set, two decision thresholds (the agreement threshold $\theta$ and the rejection threshold $\theta_{\mathrm{reject}}$), and two routing gates. Some knobs are not independent of each other, but a joint sweep on all of these knobs is wasteful. For example, when a one-to-one agreement matcher is used as the alignment strategy, the weights of another strategy become inert. The calibration therefore uses a dependency ordered structure.

The structural block of (embedder, scorer, alignment strategy) is decided with a joint sweep at default weights (E05); the weights are tuned for the surviving finalist structures (E06); the best voter subset is selected by a single-voter-drop sweep at the tuned weights of the chosen finalist structure (E06b, E06c); the full-theta grid is swept to find the best tuning for the best performing voter subset (E07); then the routing gates are ablated on the theta-calibrated best voter subset (E08); and finally, the shipped configuration's final theta curve is regenerated using the selected gate combination for the cost-quality analysis (E08b). At each stage, the earlier decisions are fixed, and the later knobs are kept at their defaults. 

\medskip{}

\textbf{Structure and weights (E05, E06).} 
All twelve combinations of embedder, scorer, and alignment strategy are evaluated in the structure screen at default weights, over a coarse $\theta$ grid. Strict-F1 and cost criteria are used, in order to select the candidate structures. The structured-similarity scorer, referred to as the hybrid scorer, beats plain embedding similarity on every embedder. This shows that the additional category, entity, and evidence-overlap signals are useful. 

The finalist structures are refined over their applicable weights and a full theta-grid sweep. The strongest combination is the Gemini embedder with the hybrid scorer under a greedy alignment strategy, using an entity-heavy blend (category/embedding/entity/evidence = 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.50 / 0.05). This structure and weight vector are fixed for the later calibration stages.

\medskip{}

\textbf{Voter-set selection (E06b, E06c).}
The initial cascade uses three Level-1 and three Level-2 voters, and escalates the unresolved cases to the Level-3 model Claude Sonnet. The Level-1 voters are \texttt{gemini-flash-lite}, \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}, and \texttt{gpt-4.1-nano}; the Level-2 voters are \texttt{gemini-flash}, \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini}, and \texttt{claude-haiku-4.5}. 

A single-voter-drop experiment is run at this stage over the full $\theta$ grid; the experiment results show that dropping \texttt{claude-haiku-4.5} reaches strict-F1 0.7147, compared with 0.7131 for the full ensemble: a difference that lies inside the bootstrap confidence interval of Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison}, while costing 18\% less per chunk.

The 5-voter cascade is selected at this stage for two reasons: first, as shown above, it is strict-F1-equivalent on the calibration set; second, and more importantly, the L2-voter Claude Haiku is from the same provider family as the silver labeler Opus, where both models are from Anthropic. Dropping it makes the L1-L2 voter ensemble completely independent of the evaluator, so the cascade's agreement with silver is no longer biased by voters belonging to the same provider. The Level-3 fallback model remains Sonnet, so this provider-independence only applies to the voter escalation signals, not to the entire cascade for the chunks that escalate to Level-3 Claude Sonnet. 

The decision of removing Claude Haiku from the cascade is made entirely on the calibration set; the generalization test of Section~\ref{section:results-generalization-to-heldout-papers} reports the results of the 5-voter cascade on a separate, held-out set of papers.

\medskip{}

\textbf{Thresholds (E07).}
With the structure, weights, and voter set fixed, the 5-voter cascade is swept over the full $\theta \times \theta_{\mathrm{reject}}$ grid at the default routing gate settings. 

The escalation of $\theta$, plotted in Figure~\ref{fig:theta-strict-f1}, has clear diminishing returns. The $\theta$-curve is monotonically increasing, and it flattens at the top. A strict-F1 gain of roughly 0.05 is observed from $\theta = 0.7$ to $0.8$, but only 0.0085 from $0.8$ to $0.9$. The rejection threshold drops the chunks with the lowest-consensus among voters; its optimal value for the 5-voter ensemble is $\theta_{\mathrm{reject}} = 0.2$.

\medskip{}

\textbf{Routing gates (E08).}
At this stage, the optimal $\theta$ value is pinned, and the two routing gates -- the single-voter policy ($\texttt{keep}$ vs $\texttt{escalate}$) and the polarity hard-fail -- are ablated on it.  After dropping Claude Haiku,  only two Level-2 voters remain, making the single-voter case at that tier more common, and \texttt{escalate} gives the maximum strict-F1 score 0.7160, compared to that of \texttt{keep} at 0.7147. Therefore, the single-voter policy of \texttt{escalate}, which sends a lone voter to Sonnet rather than trust the output, is chosen. The strict-F1 gain over \texttt{keep} is within noise, so this gate was chosen as a design decision backed by calibration, rather than as a statistically meaningful improvement. The polarity hard-fail, which decides to escalate to the more expensive tier when voters emit different polarity information, had no measurable effect.

\medskip{}

\textbf{Final configuration selection (E08b).}
After deciding on the optimum gate combination, where \texttt{escalate} was selected as the single-voter policy, the shipped configuration's $\theta$-curve is regenerated. The regeneration confirms that the shipped gate does not move the $\theta$ pin at $0.9$.

The calibration stage pins the production cascade, shown in Table~\ref{tab:abc-configuration-of-record}, and every downstream result in the thesis uses the configuration. Whether this escalation-heavy configuration is cost-effective, or whether the same quality can be obtained by running the strong model directly instead of running the 5-voter cascade, is evaluated in Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison}.
