\subsection{Section summary}\label{subsec:knowledge-section-summary}

This section described the seven-stage knowledge extraction pipeline, which converts histopathology text into typed, scored \texttt{FinalRule} records that point back to their source evidence. MAP is the only stochastic stage in the pipeline; the rest are deterministic. Each stage produces a typed object that a later stage consumes, and the design prioritizes four commitments: atomic claims, source-grounded evidence, bounded stochasticity, and auditability. 

The cost-quality calibration of MAP, which describes the voter cascade and the agreement scorer that decides whether to accept or reject the voter outputs, or escalate the text chunk to the next tier of voter models, is the subject of Section~\ref{section:Agreement_Based_Cascading}. The evaluation methodology is described in Section~\ref{section:Evaluation_Methodology}, the empirical results are given in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Results}, and the interpretation of the results is discussed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion}. Whether the pipeline succeeds at what it is designed to do is answered by those later parts.
