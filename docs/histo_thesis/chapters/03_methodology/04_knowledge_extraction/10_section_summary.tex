\subsection{Section summary}

This section described the seven-stage knowledge extraction pipeline, which converts histopathology text into typed, scored \texttt{FinalRule} records that point back to their source evidence. MAP is the only stochastic stage in the pipeline, the rest of the stages are deterministic. Each stage produces a typed object that the following stage consumes, and the design prioritizes four commitments: atomic claims, source-grounded evidence, bounded stochasticity, and auditability. 

The cost-quality calibration of MAP, which describes the voter cascade and the agreement scorer that decides whether to accept or reject the voter outputs, or escalate it to the next tier of voter models, is the subject of \pinktodo{Section 3.5, Agreement based cascading}. The evaluation methodology is described in \pinktodo{Section 3.6, Evaluation methodology}, the empirical results are given in \pinktodo{Chapter 4, Results}, and the interpretation of the results is discussed in \pinktodo{Chapter 5, Discussion}. This section has described what the knowledge-extraction pipeline is, and what it is meant to do; whether it succeeds is the subject of the following sections and chapters.
