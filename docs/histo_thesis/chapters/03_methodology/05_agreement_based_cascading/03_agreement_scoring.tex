\subsection{Agreement scoring}\label{subsec:abc-agreement-scoring}

The agreement scorer takes the set of voter outputs per chunk, and returns a score in $[0,1]$. This score measures how consistent the voters are on the underlying claim set and serves as the cascade's routing signal: it is compared against the accept and reject thresholds of Subsection~\ref{subsec:abc-accept-reject-escalate} to produce the decision.

The production scoring uses the max-consensus agreement model, adopted from~\parencite{soiffer2025semantic}. For a chunk with $N$ eligible non-empty voter outputs, the scorer constructs an $N \times N$ pairwise similarity matrix, takes each voter's mean similarity to the other voters, and reports the maximum of the means as the deferral score. In other words, the score is the mean similarity of the voter that is most aligned with the other voters. If the cascade accepts at that tier, this most-consensus-aligned voter is selected as the candidate to keep, and ties are broken by grounding quality: the fraction of findings that passed provenance validation, the average number of sources per finding, and the number of findings as a fallback.

The production configurations only differ in how pairwise similarity is calculated. The first strategy is an \texttt{embedding} strategy. 

Each finding's claim is embedded with a text-embedding model~\parencite{reimers2019sbert}. Pairwise similarity between two voters is the cosine similarity of their claim embeddings. Claims are then aligned one-to-one: each claim is matched to at most one claim from the other voter, using greedy matching in descending order of similarity. The resulting agreement score is an F1 score over the matched similarities, where precision is calculated against the first voter's claim count and recall against the second voter's. Since each claim can be used only once, unmatched claims automatically reduce the score, so over- and under-production are penalized by the matching structure itself, rather than by separate count or reuse penalties. A softer alternative was also tested during calibration, in which each claim takes its best counterpart with replacement, and explicit claim-count imbalance, reuse, and contradiction penalties are then applied. That variant was not selected; the production configuration uses the one-to-one greedy alignment (§\ref{section:results-abc-calibration}).

The alternative configuration is a \texttt{hybrid} strategy. It combines four signals over the same voter outputs using weights that sum to one: claim-embedding alignment, Jaccard overlap of finding categories, Jaccard overlap of biomedical entities in claims, and Jaccard overlap of cited text-element identifiers. The embedding-only strategy is a special case of the \texttt{hybrid} strategy, with a weight of 1 for the claim embeddings. Other weight configurations of the \texttt{hybrid} strategy test whether structured Jaccard overlaps add useful routing information beyond pure embedding similarity.

The scorer strategies and weights within are calibration parameters; their best settings on the calibration set, along with the obtained results, are reported in Section~\ref{section:results-abc-calibration}.

The agreement score is an agreement signal, not a correctness signal: a high agreement score indicates that voters returned similar claim sets; it does not imply that the consensus is correct. Agreement-based routing assumes that consensus outputs are typically more reliable than disagreement outputs.
