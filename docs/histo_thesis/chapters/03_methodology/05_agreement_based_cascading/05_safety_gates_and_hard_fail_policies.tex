\subsection{Safety gates and hard-fail policies}\label{subsec:abc-safety-gates}

The agreement scorer in Subsection~\ref{subsec:abc-agreement-scoring} only measures the similarity between the claim sets of voters; it cannot see certain contradictions between voter outputs that should override the accept-reject-escalate rule in Subsection~\ref{subsec:abc-accept-reject-escalate}. Three such safety gates are wired into the cascade for this purpose. 

The first is the polarity-conflict hard-fail. This gate fires when two voters output findings with matching subject and outcome entities, relation type, and category, but opposite polarity directions. The polarity-conflict gate is intentionally conservative: it only checks the polarity pairs with \texttt{positive} -- \texttt{negative} polarity directions, and ignores the findings with polarity direction values of \texttt{unclear}, \texttt{absent}, \texttt{partial}, and \texttt{no\_direction}.

The second safety gate is the single-voter policy, which decides the behavior of the cascade when only one voter output in a tier is eligible. This can happen when voters' API calls fail, or the voter output is rejected due to failing the provenance check. A configurable policy decides what to do in these cases: \textbf{keep} accepts the single survivor model, or \textbf{escalate} treats these single-voter situations as insufficiently supported, and escalates the chunk to the next tier.

The third safety mechanism is a schema and provenance screening step, which checks voter outputs for malformed citations and unsupported claims, before they even reach the agreement gate. 

These are conservative safety mechanisms, and do not assume that they necessarily improve strict-F1 scores. Their purpose is to prevent unsafe routing cases, such as accepting a high-consensus tier output that contains a disagreement in polarity directions. The firing frequencies, accuracy metrics for the best combination of these safety gates, as well as their calibration behavior are reported in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Results} and interpreted in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion}.
