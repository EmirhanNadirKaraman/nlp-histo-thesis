\subsection{Evaluation datasets}\label{subsec:eval-datasets}

All evaluation datasets are derived from the 977-paper ingested corpus, whose acquisition, filtering, and ingestion are described in Subsection~\ref{subsec:ingestion-counts} and summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-purpose-mapping}. Three paper subsets are drawn from the ingested corpus, and one synthetic dataset is generated; each of these is used for a different research question.

The selection, download, filtering, and ingestion procedures are described in \pinktodo{Section 3.2.2, Corpus splits and evaluation datasets}, and summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-purpose-mapping}. Two subsets are drawn from the ingested corpus: a 27-paper document-extraction evaluation dataset, and a 15-paper knowledge-extraction calibration cluster. 

The \textbf{document-extraction evaluation set} is a 27-paper sample, manually and randomly selected from the ingested corpus. It is disjoint from the knowledge-extraction subsets, in order to keep the different evaluation stages completely separate from each other. This subset is only used for the rubric-based figure and table extraction evaluation (RQ1, Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-doc-extraction}).

The \textbf{knowledge-extraction calibration cluster} is a 15-paper subset of papers, selected by an integer-linear-programming relatedness selection of Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-ilp}; its papers yield 273 knowledge-extraction source cases, and 1\,596 silver findings are extracted from these source cases by Claude Opus 4.7, at temperature zero. This cluster is only used to calibrate the knowledge-extraction thresholds and the agreement-based cascade.

The \textbf{knowledge-extraction evaluation set} is a separate subset consisting of 15 papers, drawn randomly from the corpus. It is held out from the calibration set, with silver findings generated in the same way; these findings are used to measure the strict-F1 scores of the entire knowledge-extraction pipeline (RQ2, Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-knowledge-extraction}).

Relation classification is evaluated separately on a synthetic corpus generated by Claude Opus 4.7, consisting of 300 claim-pairs, labelled as supporting, contradicting, or unrelated. This dataset is not derived from the knowledge-extraction source cases, and is used to evaluate the relation classification only (RQ4, Subsection~\ref{subsec:eval-grounding-and-relation-classification}).
