\subsection{Known limitations}\label{subsec:doc-extraction-known-limitations}

The document-extraction pipeline has several failure modes that were identified, listed here as part of the methodological scope. Chapter~\ref{chapter:Results} reports the rubric-scored incidence of the figure, table, and caption-matching failure modes. The body-text errors listed below are not captured by the rubric, and are therefore marked here separately. Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion} discusses how these limitations affect the interpretation of the downstream results. 

\begin{itemize}[label={}, leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0.4em]
    \item \textbf{Caption-attachment failures.} The caption-linking procedure discussed in Subsection~\ref{subsec:doc-table-and-figure-handling} relies on a geometric nearest-caption heuristic. This heuristic fails on rotated tables, continuation captions like "Table 1. Continued", tables with the captions attached to the sides or corners. 
    \item \textbf{Complex tables.} Multi-page or rotated tables reduce the precision of the table detection. The hybrid detector operates on one page at a time, so a multi-page table is detected as separate regions. 
    \item \textbf{Paragraph-merging and citation-stripping errors.} The syntactic stitcher (Subsection~\ref{subsec:doc-text-assembly}) can over-merge because of blocks ending with an abbreviation, and the regex citation-stripper can delete real context and information in the text. Both affect stored body text, without leaving a signal in the rubric-based evaluation.  
    \item \textbf{Downstream propagation.} Errors in text extraction propagate into the knowledge extraction pipeline: a paragraph that was never extracted cannot be a MAP finding, while a ghost-text artifact that could not be detected by the two-pass guard can be cited as evidence. The interface to the pipeline is text-only (Subsection~\ref{subsec:doc-interface-to-knowledge-pipeline}), so a mis-attached caption or a wrong crop does not enter the voter's context. The downstream pipeline applies its own grounding checks, but it cannot recover content that was not extracted. 
\end{itemize}

The strict-F1 results reported in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Results} quantify the cost of the failure modes related to figures, tables, and caption-matching. The body-text modes fall outside what the rubric measures, and therefore must be interpreted separately when observing downstream knowledge extraction results.
