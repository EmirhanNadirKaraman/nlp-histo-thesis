\subsection{Silver labels and annotation status}\label{subsec:silver-labels-and-annotation-status}

The calibration and evaluation of the knowledge-extraction pipeline do not rely on gold-label annotations by a domain expert. The reference labels, used to calibrate and evaluate the pipeline, are silver labels~\parencite{zheng2023judgingllmasajudgemtbench}: annotations generated by prompting Claude Opus 4.7, a strong language model, via a forced structured-output tool that reduces generation variance. The resulting labels approximate, through a strong LLM judge, which clinical findings should be extracted from each text chunk.

Silver labels are useful, as producing expert-curated annotations at the same scale would not be within the scope of this thesis. The silver labels enable calibration of thresholds and evaluation of the pipeline. Instead of expert annotation, the thesis uses three silver artifacts, each described in a separate subsection: the calibration finding set in Subsection~\ref{subsec:knowledge-extraction-calibration-set}, the disjoint held-out finding set in Subsection~\ref{subsec:knowledge-extraction-eval-set}, and the 300 labeled claim pairs for relation classifier evaluation in Subsection~\ref{subsec:relation-classification-eval-set}. Their roles are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-purpose-mapping}.

The finding sets and the relation set are produced in opposite directions: for the two finding sets, a silver-generation prompt was applied to each usable text chunk, and Opus 4.7 emits the findings; for the relation set, the process is reversed, and each claim pair is generated from a pre-assigned \texttt{SUPPORT}, \texttt{CONTRADICT}, or \texttt{UNRELATED} label. All these artifacts therefore support calibration and evaluation without requiring expert annotations.

None of the three datasets is treated as definitive ground truth. Since the silver labels are generated by a language model, every calibrated threshold and every measurement inherits the biases of Claude Opus 4.7: the calibrated thresholds inherit them because they are tuned on the silver calibration set, and the pipeline's strict-F1 score measurements and the relation-classification accuracy inherit them as they are scored against silver references. This bias is distinct from overfitting, which is mitigated by the disjoint calibration and held-out evaluation split.
