\subsection{Silver labels and annotation status}\label{subsec:silver-labels-and-annotation-status}

The calibration and evaluation of the knowledge-extraction pipeline does not rely on gold labels annotated by a domain expert. The reference labels, used to calibrate and evaluate the pipeline, are silver labels \pinktodo{Zheng et al. 2023}: annotations generated by prompting Claude Opus 4.7, a strong language model, under a forced structured-output tool call that reduces generation variance. The resulting labels approximate, through a strong LLM judge, which clinical findings should be extracted from each text chunk.

Silver labels are useful, as producing expert-curated annotations at the same scale would not be within the scope of this thesis. The silver-labels make it possible to calibrate the thresholds and evaluate the pipeline. Instead of expert annotation, the thesis uses three silver artifacts, each described in a separate subsection: the calibration finding set in Subsection~\ref{subsec:knowledge-extraction-calibration-set}, the disjoint held-out finding set in Subsection~\ref{subsec:knowledge-extraction-eval-set}, and the 300 labeled claim pairs for relation classifier evaluation in Subsection~\ref{subsec:relation-classification-eval-set}. Their roles are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset-purpose-mapping}.

All silver findings and labeled claim pairs are generated by Claude Opus 4.7. To produce the two finding sets, a silver-generation prompt was applied to each usable text chunk, as explained in Subsection~\ref{subsec:knowledge-extraction-eval-set}. The relation-classification set is generated from given labels: the claim pairs are generated, based on a supporting, contradicting, or unrelated label. These artifacts provide the data for calibration and evaluation of the pipeline components, without requiring expert annotations.

None of the three datasets are treated as definitive ground truth. Since the silver labels are generated by a language model, every calibrated threshold and every measurement inherits the biases of Claude Opus 4.7: the calibrated thresholds inherit them because they are tuned on the silver calibration set, and the pipeline strict-F1 score measurements and the relation-classification accuracy inherit them as they are scored against silver references. Overfitting, however, is not a concern for either evaluation: the knowledge-extraction and relation-classification evaluation datasets are both disjoint from the calibration set. 

The synthetic-pair caveat noted in Subsection~\ref{subsec:relation-classification-eval-set}, namely that the synthetic claim pairs contain cleaner relations than the corpus and therefore have limited generalizability to real claims, is discussed further in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion}.
