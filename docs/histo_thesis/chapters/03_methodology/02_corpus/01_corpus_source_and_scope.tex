The corpus used in this thesis is drawn from the Open Access subset of PubMed Central, maintained by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. PMC OA is suitable for this thesis for three reasons: first, every article in this subset is made available under a Creative Commons or similar license, allowing for data processing, redistribution, and analysis at scale. Second, each article is available in the form of a PDF, and where the publisher supplied it, also in a JATS-XML format; even though this thesis does not make use of XML files, they can also be used as a supplementary tool in the document extraction pipeline, as discussed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion}. Third, PMC OA supplies a big selection of biomedical papers, from which a large enough subset of histopathology-focused papers could be selected. 

In this thesis, we used papers written in English whose content is relevant to histopathology. The target papers are those with subject matters centering on tissue-level pathology findings, biomarker expressions, morphological assessments, staging, diagnostic interpretations, prognostic associations, and related biomedical claims. The scope of selected papers is deliberately narrow, and is chosen to provide a focused dataset to evaluate the thesis system on histopathology-oriented information extraction. We do not claim that the selected corpus is representative of all biomedical knowledge, nor do we claim that the results obtained in this thesis transfer directly to other biomedical subfields.
