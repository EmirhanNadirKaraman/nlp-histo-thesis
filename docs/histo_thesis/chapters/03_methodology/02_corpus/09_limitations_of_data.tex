\subsection{Limitations of the data}\label{subsec:corpus-limitations-of-data}

The data design in this thesis has several limitations, which are stated explicitly below. They bound how the results reported in Chapter~\ref{chapter:Results} should be interpreted; Chapter~\ref{chapter:Discussion} discusses their implications on the validity of those results.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Histopathology focus.} This corpus is filtered to contain only histopathology papers. Therefore, no claims can be made about the results being representative of biomedical literature as a whole, or other biomedical subfields.
    
    \item \textbf{English-language only.} In practice, the ingested corpus is English-only: the histopathology keyword filtering on the PMC Open Access Subset yields English articles, and papers in other languages were not targeted. Therefore, this thesis does not evaluate whether the document and knowledge-extraction pipeline results transfer to non-English histopathology literature.
    
    \item \textbf{PMC Open Access bias.} The corpus is drawn from the PMC Open Access papers, which might not represent the full histopathology literature. Consequently, the figure conventions, table structures, layout patterns, and caption styles reflect the article types in that subset. 
    
    \item \textbf{Silver-label limits.} The knowledge-extraction reference labels are LLM-generated rather than expert-annotated, so these labels inherit biases of the silver-label generators (Subsection~\ref{subsec:silver-labels-and-annotation-status}). Furthermore, the voter-agreement system (Section~\ref{section:Agreement_Based_Cascading}) scores voters by cosine-similarity between embeddings, potentially inheriting biases from the embedding models that were used.
    
\end{itemize}
