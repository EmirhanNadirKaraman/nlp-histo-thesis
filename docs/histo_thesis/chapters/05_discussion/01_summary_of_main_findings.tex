\section{Summary of main findings}\label{section:disc-summary-of-findings}

Five findings are discussed in this chapter. First, the document-extraction pipeline achieves a strict-F1 score of $0.838$ for tables and $0.840$ on figures, over the 27-paper rubric set and a four-dimensional rubric, while preserving full provenance on the full ingested corpus (Sections~\ref{section:results-doc-extraction-performance},~\ref{section:results-ke-pipeline-output-and-provenance}). Second, the knowledge-extraction pipeline yields a fine-grained, source-grounded rule base: the MAP-stage achieves $0.7160$ strict-F1; the quality and structure of its outputs are preserved on a held-out set of 15 papers (Sections~\ref{section:results-ke-pipeline-output-and-provenance},~\ref{section:results-generalization-to-heldout-papers}). Third, the calibrated cascade reaches the quality of the strongest single-model baseline and substantially beats every other single-model baseline, but it is not cost-justified under the silver-label metric against running the strongest model alone (Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison}). Fourth, the grounding filter and the relation classifier share a single NLI model, and the grounding threshold is set to achieve stronger source faithfulness, at the expense of a lower strict-F1 (Section~\ref{section:results-nli-in-grounding-and-relation-classification}). Fifth, the configuration frozen after calibration generalizes well to held-out papers, without clear signs of overfitting (Section~\ref{section:results-generalization-to-heldout-papers}). The following sections discuss these points in order, then discuss the threats that bound the interpretation of results, and their implications.
