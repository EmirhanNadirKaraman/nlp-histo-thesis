\section{Knowledge extraction quality and stability}\label{section:disc-knowledge-extraction-quality-and-stability}

\textbf{Quality.} The findings extracted by the cascade in the MAP-stage of the knowledge-extraction pipeline are evaluated against Opus silver labels; a strict-F1 score of $0.7160$ is achieved on the calibration set (\texttt{related15}), and $0.7128$ on the disjoint, held-out evaluation set (\texttt{heldout15}), the results for which are reported in more detail in Table~\ref{tab:results-heldout-map-generalization}. These scores refer to the MAP-stage output before the grounding filter: in Section~\ref{section:disc-grounding-polarity-conflicts-and-safety-gates}, the decision to use a grounding filter for source faithfulness at the expense of a lower strict-F1 score is discussed. At this operating point, the extractor recovers most Opus-labeled findings, achieving $0.90$ recall and $0.872$ precision. The remaining gap to a perfect silver-agreement is bounded not only by the cascade, but also by the biases of the silver labeler itself (Section~\ref{section:disc-threats-to-validity}). 

\medskip{}

\textbf{The pipeline does not compress; it extracts and grounds.} The funnel in Table~\ref{tab:knowledge-extraction-funnel} shows that the NORMALIZE, GROUP, and CANONICALIZE stages retain almost everything: the pipeline produces roughly one final rule per normalized finding, with a consolidation ratio of $93.9\%$, and does not collapse many findings into a few. This is a direct consequence of the MAP prompt, which asks voters to output atomic claims. The system output is a large, granular, and traceable rule base (116.5 rules per paper); the system should therefore not be interpreted as a summarizer. 

\medskip{}

\textbf{Stability.} The generalization test on the held-out set (RQ5) provides the strongest evidence in the thesis that the measured results are not due to overfitting on the calibration set. MAP's strict-F1 falls from $0.7160$ to $0.7128$ on the held-out set; the strict-F1 difference is only $0.0032$, comparable to the bootstrap noise band reported in Section~\ref{section:results-cost-quality-comparison}. The downstream funnel also retains its shape, where the consolidation ratio moves from $93.9\%$ to $94.9\%$, a difference of only about one percentage point (Table~\ref{tab:results-heldout-ke-funnel}). Therefore, there is no meaningful overfitting to the calibration set of \texttt{related15}. 

Two additional observations also transfer from the calibration set to the held-out set. First, the escalation rate stays very high ($0.956 \rightarrow 0.990$), reproducing the finding of Section~\ref{section:disc-agreement-based-cascading} that the quality point of the cascade behaves almost the same as a single-Sonnet system. The relation output also stays sparse ($25 \rightarrow 4$ relations, no held-out contradictions). The relation sparsity is a property of the corpus: related papers very rarely state directly comparable claims, and a randomly sampled set of papers (as in the evaluation set) states even fewer; this is the exact reason why the relation-classifier was evaluated on a synthetic balanced set, as discussed in Section~\ref{section:disc-nli-as-shared-backbone}.
