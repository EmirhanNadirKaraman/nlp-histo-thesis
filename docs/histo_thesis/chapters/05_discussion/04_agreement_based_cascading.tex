\section{Agreement-based cascading}\label{section:disc-agreement-based-cascading}

The conclusion for the agreement-based cascading system is deliberately stated up-front: the calibrated cascade is not cost-justified against a single strong model, under the silver cost-quality metric. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:results-cascade-vs-single-model-baselines}, the pinned five-voter cascade achieves a $0.7160$ strict-F1 score with a $95.6\%$ escalation rate; single-Sonnet reaches $0.7129$. The difference is $0.0031$, and its 95\% confidence interval of $[-0.0002, +0.0068]$ contains zero, which was calculated by a paper-level bootstrap described in Table~\ref{tab:results-bootstrap-comparison-cascade-single-models}; the five-voter cascade and single-Sonnet are therefore statistically indistinguishable in quality, while single-Sonnet is roughly $24\%$ cheaper, at $18.00$ versus $23.66$ per chunk. For readers who can afford Sonnet, and consider silver-agreement as their only criterion, running Sonnet alone is the better choice. The value of the cascade is not fully measured by silver-agreement.

\medskip{}

\textbf{Cascade is clearly stronger than all cheaper alternatives.} The same comparison in Table~\ref{tab:results-cascade-vs-single-model-baselines} shows that the cascade beats every other single model baseline, statistically confirmed by the bootstrap as significant: $+0.060$ over single Haiku, $+0.120$ over GPT-4.1-mini, and between $+0.14$ and $+0.26$ over the remaining four models. The cascade is only dominated at the top of the cost axis by the strong single-Sonnet, which is from the same model family as the silver-label model Opus; everywhere else it is the quality leader. 

The cost-quality frontier detailed in Table~\ref{tab:results-cascade-cost-quality-frontier} and Figure~\ref{fig:cost-quality-plot} shows the trade-off clearly. Strict-F1 increases from $0.5433$ (economy) to $0.7067$ (knee) to $0.7160$ (quality), but the knee-to-quality step raises the strict-F1 by only one point with $9\%$ extra cost; this indicates that most of the attainable quality is already reached before the cascade escalates almost everything to the highest tier. In other words, cascade gets its quality from escalating hard chunks to Sonnet, which is why the best configuration of the cascade converges to the single-Sonnet baseline. 

\medskip{}

\textbf{Cascade's value the metric cannot see.} The structure of the cascade causes an important limitation, making the gap measured between the cascade and the single-Sonnet a conservative one: the silver-labels are produced by Opus, while the strongest model in the cascade is Sonnet, both of which are from the same model family. A single-Sonnet system, therefore, may benefit from the similarity of its output shape and judgment to the silver output. Interestingly, the voter-subset selection of Section~\ref{section:results-abc-calibration} drops Haiku from the second tier of the cascade, which results in a strict-F1 difference within the confidence interval at $18\%$ lower per-chunk cost, compared to the full ensemble; all models involved in the cascade's decision process in the first two tiers are therefore non-Anthropic -- though the Level-3 fallback remains Sonnet, so this independence only applies to the first two tiers that control voting. This causes the cascade findings to reflect agreement between voters from different providers, which is the type of evidence Opus's silver labels are least likely to reward. 

Therefore, the fair conclusion we arrive at is that, under an Opus-based silver standard, the cascade's true quality is possibly understated, and the results should not be interpreted as evidence that single-Sonnet is better than the cascade. A human-judged metric could narrow, eliminate, or even reverse the observed gap. 

\medskip{}

\textbf{The framework is the lasting contribution.} The reported cost-quality numbers are tied to this corpus, the chosen voter pool, and their pricing at the time of the evaluation. The implemented cascade framework and its core components -- calibrated agreement scoring, accept/reject/escalate routing per text chunk, the single-voter and polarity gates discussed in Section~\ref{section:disc-grounding-polarity-conflicts-and-safety-gates}, and the offline-replay calibration protocol -- can be reused with different voter models, scorers, and pricing. 

For that reason, the thesis argues that pipelines involving a cascade architecture with calibrated cost-quality knobs should be used: with a cheaper, stronger, or more diverse voter pool, more favorable points can be identified on the frontier, possibly exceeding the performance observed on this pipeline. The contribution of this thesis, therefore, is not only the selected operating point, but the calibration and evaluation procedures that allow for finding such an operating point, and measuring its performance.
