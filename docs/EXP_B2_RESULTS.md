## 2. Family-bias diagnostic

The cost-quality picture above raises a methodological question: silver
was generated by Claude Opus 4.7 (Anthropic). Are the strong Anthropic
results (Haiku, Sonnet at the top) a real capability difference, or is
the OpenAI matcher embedder (`text-embedding-3-small`) systematically
rewarding Anthropic-family phrasing?

The diagnostic in `scripts/eval/b2_family_bias_diagnostic.py` answers this
with two statistics per voter, both replay-only over cached embeddings:

* **`mean_matched_sim`** — average cosine over matched (≥ 0.55) pairs.
  Bias inside the threshold band → bias here.
* **`mean_best_per_silver_sim`** — for every silver finding, the maximum
  cosine to any of that voter's findings (threshold-free). Bias in how
  the embedder positions claim texts in vector space → bias here.

### What the diagnostic shows

**(1) gpt-4.1-mini's matched pairs are MORE similar to silver than Haiku's** —
0.895 vs 0.889. This is the single most damaging data point for the
family-bias hypothesis. If silver-from-Opus rewarded Anthropic phrasing,
Anthropic matches should be cleanest. They are not — when gpt-4.1-mini
matches, it matches *as well as Sonnet* (within 0.012).

**(2) Anthropic–OpenAI family deltas are inside the noise band.**

| metric | Δ (Anthropic − OpenAI) | evidence threshold (0.10·sim_threshold) |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean_matched_sim | +0.027 | 0.055 |
| mean_best_per_silver_sim | +0.048 | 0.055 |

Both deltas fall below the 0.055 threshold for "take as evidence." The
diagnostic is consistent with no family bias.

**(3) The 2.8pp Haiku-over-gpt-4.1-mini strict_f1 advantage is recall, not
embedder-favoritism.**
- Haiku: 1136 matches, mean similarity 0.889
- gpt-4.1-mini: 1085 matches, mean similarity 0.895

Haiku wins on count of matches (recall), losing on per-match similarity.
The matcher is not treating Haiku findings more generously — it is just
that Haiku produces more findings that pass the 0.55 threshold.

**(4) Gemini's collapse is structural, not family.** mean_best = 0.554-0.557
vs OpenAI's 0.788-0.877 — Gemini findings sit far from silver in the
embedding space across the entire distribution, not just at the matched
zone. Combined with their massive over-generation (n_pipeline = 1899 /
2235 vs silver's 1243), this is **schema/style mismatch on this clinical
text**, not a family-bias artifact. Gemini-on-AuditableSummary needs its
own investigation, separate from the cost-quality story.

**(5) Sonnet's lead over Haiku (4.2pp strict_f1) is real capability.** Both
Anthropic — family bias cannot explain a within-family gap.

## 3. Conclusions

**The EXP B.2 cost-effectiveness ranking is robust to the family-bias
challenge.** The headline findings:

* **Haiku alone at $0.0036/chunk** achieves strict_f1 = 0.696 — 73 %
  cheaper than Sonnet for a 4.2pp accuracy cost. Genuine result.
* **gpt-4.1-mini alone at $0.0016/chunk** achieves strict_f1 = 0.668 — 88 %
  cheaper than Sonnet, and its matched-pair similarity is the highest of
  any voter tested (0.895). The cheapest *deployable* strong model on this
  task.
* **The cascade's +0.005 strict_f1 over Sonnet-only for +51 % cost** is a
  marginal-value trade. Bootstrap CI (EXP A, currently stubbed) would
  determine whether the gap is statistically separable.

**Recommended thesis framing.** The cascade is the calibrated architecture
(rigorous ABC tuning is the methodological contribution); however, the
cost-effective deployable system is a **single mid-tier voter**:
gpt-4.1-mini for aggressive cost minimisation, Haiku for a balanced
cost/quality point. Sonnet-only is the strong-model ceiling. The thesis
should present all four as deployment options, with the cascade earning
its premium only if the +0.005 strict_f1 lift over Sonnet-only is
demonstrably real (pending EXP A bootstrap CI).

## 4. Open questions / follow-ups

* **Run `--split test`.** Confirm the dev-tuned ranking survives held-out
  test. Cache is now warm from the dev run; expect mostly hits.
  ```bash
  python scripts/eval/run_summarization_experiments.py --phase baselines --run --split test
  python scripts/eval/b2_family_bias_diagnostic.py --split test
  ```
* **EXP A (bootstrap CI).** The cascade-vs-Sonnet 0.005 lift needs a CI to
  decide whether it survives resampling noise. Currently stubbed
  (`scripts/eval/run_summarization_experiments.py::_run_exp_a` returns a
  recipe). Implementation needs `--keep-per-case` plumbing in
  `eval/silver/map_theta_sweep.run_sweep` so per-case match outputs are
  retained for resampling.
* **Gemini-on-AuditableSummary failure-mode audit.** Both Flash and
  Flash-Lite over-generate (1899-2235 findings vs target 1243) and produce
  outputs that the OpenAI embedder positions far from silver. Sample 20
  Gemini findings and check whether they're (a) hallucinating extra
  findings, (b) producing valid findings in a different style, or (c)
  failing AuditableSummary schema validation in subtle ways.
* **Replicating with a non-Anthropic silver oracle.** Although the
  family-bias diagnostic above is negative, an even cleaner test would
  regenerate silver from a non-Anthropic strong model (gpt-4.1 or
  gemini-2.5-pro) and re-run B.2. Cost: ~$6 silver regeneration + free
  replay. Optional belt-and-braces — current data already supports the
  thesis claim.

## 5. How to reproduce

```bash
# Step 0 — ensure voter cache + silver + OPENAI_API_KEY are in place.
ls eval/data/map_primer/voter_cache.json eval/data/silver_findings.jsonl
echo "OPENAI_API_KEY set: $([ -n "$OPENAI_API_KEY" ] && echo yes || echo no)"

# Step 1 — EXP B.2 baseline CSV
python scripts/eval/run_summarization_experiments.py --phase baselines --run

# Step 2 — family-bias diagnostic CSV
python scripts/eval/b2_family_bias_diagnostic.py --split dev
```

Outputs land in `eval/reports/exp_b2_*.csv`. The orchestrator additionally
appends a manifest row at `eval/reports/manifests/manifest_<ts>.json`.
Neither writes to `summarization_experiment_state.json` — by design,
diagnostic phases do not mutate the BEST_/FINAL_/PROVISIONAL_FINAL_ keys.
